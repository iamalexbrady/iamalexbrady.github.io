## 1. I am watching.

Search your mind for a minute or so with eyes closed, and actively seek not to overlook any “little” thought that may tend to elude the search.
This watching will take some getting used to. The difficulty is found in not making artificial distinctions.
Every thought that occurs to you, regardless of the qualities that you assign to it, is a suitable subject. It is the objective to "see" each thought.

In the practice periods, first repeat the idea to yourself: "I am watching."

Then as a thought crosses your mind, hold it in awareness while you tell yourself:

I see this thought about _____.

Five, two-minute practice periods are recommended. Three is enough.

The practice periods can be spaced over a day or an hour.

**Take it Live:**

While our practice exercises are initially learned as a meditative tool,
significant gains are captured when the mental exercise is performed within the "hustle & bustle" of daily life.
Take this exercise into daily life with the following routine:

Use "I am watching" if you become aware of a particular thought that arouses uneasiness.
The following form is suggested for this purpose:

I see this thought about _____.
