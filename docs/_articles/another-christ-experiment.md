Another Christ experiment:

**Turn the Sound Off**

You should be able to enter into a state defined by the absence of
the sense of sound.

It is likely the reseacher will first notice the sensation as
having happened.

"Was the sound off for that last period of time? I think so."
After having noticed the effect is possible,
the researcher can continue on in learning how to intentionally
accomplish the ability to control the perception of sound.

---

**Turn the Weather Off**

You should be able to enter into a state defined by the absence of
the sense of temperature.

It is likely the researcher will first notice the sensation has
having happened.

"I felt the temperature enough to have commented on it an hour ago,
but then I experienced a period of time where the temperature
was not apparent or important at all."

After having noticed the effect is possible,
the researcher can continue on in learning how to intentionally
accomplish the ability to control the perception of temperature.

---

Why is the second one called "Turn the weather off" instead of
"Turn the temperature off"?

We can turn the weather off, too.

Why is the title singular "Experiment," yet this article lists two?

The singular expeiment is "Turn off physical sensation."
In the state of realization, one sees the primacy of physicality is illusory.
Primacy is of mind. One can begin loosening the belief in physicality
piecewise through experiments like the ones listed. Eventually, in one go,
all senses are seen to be a secondary effect, and "turned off" if desired.
